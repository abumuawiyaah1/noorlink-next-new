## Post 2 — Entry & ESTA (know before you go)

**Status:** `review`  
**Image:** `nyc-hub-entry-docs`  
**Platforms:** Meta + LinkedIn

### Meta

```
U.S. entry, in plain English:

• Many UK/EU (and other Visa Waiver) passports → ESTA
• Not on the list, or longer/different trip → visa route
• Even some connections still need authorization

Apply ESTA at least 72 hours before departure (a week or two is calmer).

At immigration, have ready:
Passport · ESTA/visa proof · return plan · first-night address

Always confirm on official government sites — rules change.

#ESTA #USTravel #TravelTips #NYC
```

### LinkedIn

```
For inbound trips to New York, entry paperwork is the step that cannot be improvised at the gate.

Visa Waiver travelers typically use ESTA (apply well before departure — 72 hours minimum, earlier if you want buffer). Other passports follow the consular visa path. Eligibility follows the passport country, which matters for many Middle East–based travelers holding different nationalities.

At CBP: passport, authorization proof, onward ticket, and a clear first address. Short, consistent answers work better than long stories.

Verify current rules on official U.S. government sites before you fly.

#BusinessTravel #USTravel #ESTA #NYC
```

## Post 3 — Money & tipping norms

**Status:** `review`  
**Image:** `nyc-hub-payments`  
**Platforms:** Meta + LinkedIn

### Meta

```
NYC money, simply:

• Cards / contactless for most everyday spend
• Keep some USD for tips and edge cases
• Tax is often added at the register
• Sit-down meals: tipping 15–20% is common if service isn’t included

Tell your bank you’re traveling. Apple Pay / Google Pay help if your card is loaded.

Emergencies: 911 · City info: 311

#NYC #TravelTips #MoneyAbroad
```

### LinkedIn

```
Payment norms are a quiet source of inbound friction in New York.

Contactless cards cover most restaurants, transit, and hotels. Visitors still want a little USD for tips and occasional cash-only edges. Sales tax is typically added at checkout, and sit-down service often expects a 15–20% tip when not included.

Load your wallet on your phone before you fly, notify your bank, and keep embassy + insurer numbers offline.

#BusinessTravel #NYC #TravelTips
```

## Post 4 — Airport to hotel (first 2 hours)

**Status:** `review`  
**Image:** `nyc-hub-airport-exit`  
**Platforms:** Meta + LinkedIn  
**Priority:** Strong Meta carousel frame / LinkedIn single

### Meta

```
JFK → Manhattan without the scramble:

1. Immigration, bags, customs
2. Official ground transport only (taxi line, rideshare zone, or AirTrain)
3. Exact hotel address in Maps

Rideshare needs working data. AirTrain + subway works with light bags and a plan. Yellow taxis: use the official stand.

First two hours goal: hotel, water, simple food — then the city.

#JFK #NYC #TravelTips #AirportTransfer
```

### LinkedIn

```
Airport exit is an operations problem, not a sightseeing problem.

From JFK, choose before you land: AirTrain + transit (budget, light luggage), official taxi (clear process), or rideshare (door-to-door — requires mobile data and the correct pickup zone). Ignore informal solicitations inside the terminal.

Have the first address ready to paste into Maps. The win condition for hour one is simple: bags down, water, food, connection working.

#BusinessTravel #NYC #JFK #TravelLogistics
```

**Status:** For review — do not publish as a live page until signed off in `REVIEW.md`

**Job of this hub:** Clear the first two hours after landing — entry, money, transit, and phone data

## Introduction

Most arrival friction in New York is front-loaded: immigration questions, how you pay, how you leave the airport, and whether your phone has working data.

If entry paperwork is done before departure, you know how payments work, you’ve chosen an airport exit method, and mobile data is already set up, the city becomes manageable quickly. Use this hub as a pre-departure checklist, then keep it bookmarked for landing day.

> Official rules change. Confirm visas/entry and airport transit on government and airport sites before you fly.

## 1. Entry & Visa Logistics

### Plain-English entry rules (tourist / short visit)

| Your situation | Typical path | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK, most EU Schengen nationals, and other Visa Waiver Program (VWP) passports | **ESTA** | Short tourism/business stays (generally up to 90 days). Authorization to board and request entry — not a visa. |
| Passport not on VWP, or trip outside VWP rules | **B-1/B-2** (or other visa) | Consular application; longer lead time. |
| Airside transit only | Usually still needs ESTA or a visa | “Just connecting” often still requires authorization. |

**Middle East:** Eligibility follows your **passport country**, not where you live. Some travelers use a VWP second nationality; others need a visa. Check the official Visa Waiver list for your passport.

**UK / Europe:** ESTA is the common tourism path. Expired or refused ESTA means you generally cannot board under VWP — fix it before airport day.

### Recommended timelines

- **ESTA:** At least **72 hours** before departure; **1–2 weeks** ahead is calmer if something needs fixing.
- **Embassy visa:** Start **weeks to months** ahead, based on local appointment wait times.
- **Passport:** Must be valid for your stay; VWP has extra passport rules (e.g. e-passport). Confirm on official ESTA / CBP pages.

### What to present at primary immigration (CBP)

- Passport used for ESTA or visa  
- ESTA approval or visa evidence  
- Return or onward ticket  
- Address for first nights (hotel, rental, or host)  
- Clear purpose of trip in one sentence  

Short, consistent answers work better than long stories. For customs: declare restricted items; food rules are strict — when unsure, declare.

## 2. Money, Payments & Local Norms

### Card vs cash

- Cards dominate restaurants, transit, hotels, and most shops. Contactless Visa/Mastercard is widely accepted.
- Practical visitor split: **most everyday spend can be card**; keep **some USD cash** for tips, small vendors, and terminal failures.
- Tell your bank you’re traveling. Prefer a card with no foreign-transaction fee if you have one.

### Tipping, tax, and small-cash scenarios

- **Sales tax** is usually added at the register — shelf price is not always final.
- **Tipping (typical service culture):**  
  - Sit-down restaurants: often **15–20%** pre-tax if service isn’t included  
  - Counters / cafés: optional small tip  
  - Taxi / rideshare: optional; many tip **10–15%** for solid trips  
  - Housekeeping / porters: small cash if you tip  
- Cash still helps for tip envelopes, some markets, and outages.

### Apps and emergency contacts

- **Apple Pay / Google Pay** — useful if your foreign card is loaded  
- **Venmo / Zelle** — common locally; optional for short inbound trips  
- **911** — police / fire / medical  
- **311** — NYC non-emergency info  
- Save your **embassy/consulate** and travel-insurer 24/7 number as screenshots offline

## 3. City & Regional Highlights

Practical picks — iconic, workable on a normal budget. Re-check hours and prices before you go.

### Dollar-slice pizza (Midtown & downtown counters)

- **Vibe / price:** Fast, standing or quick seat; very low cost per meal  
- **Consensus:** A “landed in NYC” ritual — not fine dining, reliable when hungry  
- **Pro tip:** Use it for **day-one hunger** after the airport when you don’t want a restaurant wait  

**Image:** `nyc-hub-food-slice`

### Staten Island Ferry

- **Vibe / price:** Deck views of harbor / Statue of Liberty area; **passenger fare is free**  
- **Consensus:** High-value sightseeing without a ticketed cruise  
- **Pro tip:** Stand on the **right side leaving Manhattan** for classic views; go off-peak for fewer crowds  

**Image:** `nyc-hub-ferry`

### The High Line (Chelsea / Hudson Yards)

- **Vibe / price:** Elevated park walk; free to enter. Nearby food ranges casual to spendy  
- **Consensus:** Easy first-afternoon activity when jet-lagged  
- **Pro tip:** Walk **one direction only**; pair with a deli or grocery run instead of a long dinner if you’re tired

## 4. Arrival Transit & First 2 Hours

### Airports

- **JFK** — common for long-haul from Europe / Middle East / UK  
- **EWR (Newark)** — also major international  
- **LGA** — more short-haul; less common for this inbound profile  

### Transit vs rideshare vs taxi

| Option | Best when | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AirTrain + subway / NJ Transit | Light bags, daytime, budget | Stairs/transfers; learn the map before landing |
| Official yellow taxi | Tired, luggage, clear process | Use official taxi lines only |
| Rideshare (Uber/Lyft) | Door-to-door with app ready | Needs **working data** + correct pickup zone |
| Hotel / private transfer | Groups, late night, low stress | Book ahead; confirm terminal |

### JFK → Manhattan (typical flow)

1. Immigration → bags → customs.  
2. Follow **Ground Transportation** signs. Ignore informal “taxi” solicitors inside the terminal.  
3. Choose AirTrain + subway, official taxi, or rideshare pickup per airport signs.  
4. Have your **exact hotel address** ready for Maps.

### Paying for local transit

- **OMNY:** many buses/subways take a **contactless bank card or phone wallet tap**.  
- Budget separately for **JFK AirTrain** fare where it applies.

### First 2 hours checklist

1. Immigration → baggage → customs  
2. Small card/cash test (water or transit)  
3. Exit mode to hotel  
4. Drop bags → water + simple food → then sightseeing

## 5. Mobile Connectivity & eSIM Hardware

### Avoid airport kiosk SIM markups

Airport SIM counters are convenient and often expensive per GB. Installing connectivity **before you fly** skips the queue and the “no signal while hunting Wi‑Fi” loop.

### Diagnostic compatibility test (at home)

1. Open the Phone dialer.  
2. Dial **`*#06#`**.  
3. Confirm an **EID** appears (with IMEI). An EID means the phone can hold an eSIM profile.  
4. In Settings, confirm **Add eSIM** / **Add cellular plan**.  
5. Confirm the phone is **carrier-unlocked**.

### Recommended dual-line setup

| Line | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Primary (home) | WhatsApp identity, banking **SMS 2FA**, voice if needed |
| Secondary (travel eSIM) | **Mobile data** for maps, rideshare, messaging data |

**On landing:** set mobile data to the travel line; enable data roaming on that line as your plan requires; keep primary active for SMS. Test Maps and rideshare before you leave airport Wi‑Fi if you can.

For NYC, pickup zones, OMNY help, and sharing location with your hotel all assume **working data in hour one**.
